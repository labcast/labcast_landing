# Labcast — You film. Labcast broadcasts.

## Creative brief

**Format:** full-screen illustrated hero with the headline and signup layered over the artwork, followed by seven scroll-driven chapters, one continuous pinned presentation on portrait phones and tall screens; a linear illustrated story for reduced motion, no JavaScript, and short landscape windows. Desktop uses large artwork beside a short caption. Mobile stacks the caption and artwork. The feature story has no timed autoplay, progress bar, timer, or play button. The separate event marquee moves automatically.

A small crew wants to share a neighborhood game. They bring their phones; Labcast takes over production. The same cobalt-shirted player, coral-jacketed camera operator, orange basketball, and peach court continue through the story. A wide crew view becomes an incoming feed, then a selected broadcast, replay, and finished show. The final illustration shifts to an older couple celebrating at home, with Twitch, YouTube, TikTok, and Instagram shown as planned direct destinations. “Broadcast like a pro” provides the closing payoff. The emotional journey is anticipation → relief → delight → connection.

**Primary promise:** You bring the cameras. Labcast handles the broadcast. Attribute the work to Labcast in public copy. Explain the outcome rather than repeatedly foregrounding AI. Camera guidance is optional and secondary; following directions must never appear necessary for broadcasting. A person can take control, but does not have to operate the production constantly.

**Art direction:** expressive editorial cartoons with confident varied ink lines, warm paper grain, dynamic silhouettes, and a restrained cream, orange, lime, cobalt, and peach palette. Preserve the neo-brutalist page identity. The founder reviewed the generated illustrations positively. Continue their style rather than reverting to flat geometric people or stock-photo panels.

## Production prompts and assets

The exact prompts, generation mode, and saved source paths are in [prompts.md](prompts.md). Built-in image generation produced four separate 1536 × 1024 artworks: `crew.png`, `action.png`, `reaction.png`, and `viewers.png`. The first image established identity and style; subsequent images used it as the visual reference. UI graphics, framing, captions, and motion remain code-native and editable.

Combined direction for future artwork: preserve the same fictional neighborhood game, expressive hand-drawn cartoon style, ink contours, paper grain, cream sky, orange ball, cobalt player, coral camera operator, and peach court. Vary the viewpoint to support the specific story beat. End on the people who benefit from watching elsewhere. Generate clean artwork without text; keep typography and interface labels in the website. Do not substitute unrelated stock imagery for simultaneous camera angles.

## Quality checks and references

Avoid generic clipart, simple geometric people, photoreal substitutes, inconsistent characters, empty decorative motion, dense explanatory diagrams, fictitious performance statistics, and generated interface lettering. Do not describe Labcast using development-origin stories, prototypes, or pilots. Hackathons are an intended event category and appear in the event marquee. Keep the waitlist stage clear without repeatedly interrupting the narrative.

Check each of the seven holds on desktop and mobile, reverse scrolling, transitions between holds, pin release, reduced motion, keyboard access, short windows, resize, and the final CTA. Every feature must remain available without motion. Screenshot evidence is in [the storyboard viewer](storyboard.html) and [preview/story.json](preview/story.json).

Reviewed skills: [Apple Design](https://github.com/bowen31337/apple-design/blob/main/SKILL.md), its [scroll guidance](https://github.com/bowen31337/apple-design/blob/main/references/gsap.md), and [Storyboard Video Previs](https://github.com/gainubi/storyboard-video-skill/blob/main/SKILL.md). Applied principles: reversible scroll-linked transitions, one visual purpose per chapter, spatial continuity, distinct framing, and browser review. Existing Labcast colors and typography take precedence over the Apple skill’s UI-token defaults. No third-party install scripts were run. Implementation uses the documented [GSAP ScrollTrigger](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/) API.

## Hero and motion revision

On desktop, the headline, promise, and signup form one vertically centered group with a slightly larger headline. The signup follows the promise with a fixed 22px gap. Mobile keeps its upper placement to leave the illustration visible below. The second headline line uses a lime block, heavy ink outline, and hard offset shadow. The orange signup button and heavier borders strengthen the neo-brutalist direction.

A separate “Every kind of live” section immediately below the hero contains the edge-to-edge event marquee. It replaces the green audience strip, so the hero focuses on the headline, artwork, and signup. Five illustrated cards show gaming streams, live music, club sports, product demos, and hackathons. One bubble follows the card nearest the marquee center. It takes 35 seconds to cycle the full set. The loop pauses on hover, keyboard focus, leaving the viewport, and a hidden tab; reduced motion and no JavaScript expose a native horizontal list. The cards retain their own frames; there is no outer panel around the track. Cards are 225px wide on phones and scale up on desktop. A 74px upper gutter keeps the speech bubbles inside the unclipped area. There are no playback buttons. Small screens also place the full-width marquee in its own section below the hero.

The hero has a staggered entrance and scroll-driven artwork movement. The story uses native wheel, trackpad, touch, and keyboard scrolling, with one viewport of reading space per chapter. There is no input interception, cooldown, forced snapping, or gesture lock. A large swipe can pass multiple chapters, as approved by the founder. One persistent broadcast layer stays on screen: the action image is the same DOM element through broadcasting, shot selection, replays, and graphics. Only a changed camera source crossfades (400ms); the outgoing source remains opaque underneath. Feature overlays fade separately. Copy fades out for 140ms before the next caption fades in for 240ms, avoiding overlapping headlines. All fades complete on their own clock even when scrolling stops. Reduced motion, no JavaScript, and short landscape screens retain the complete linear story. Portrait phones keep the animated story below 700px; captions and artwork adapt to the available height. Touch layouts use a native inner scroller under a fixed header, with the outer paper background stationary to avoid Safari status-bar bleed. Desktop keeps native document scrolling. The screen-reader transcript remains. Artwork prompts: [marquee-prompts.md](marquee-prompts.md).

### Apple reference checked October 16, 2026

Inspected the live [iPhone Pro page](https://www.apple.com/iphone-18-pro/), its `overview.built.css`, and `overview/main.built.js`. The page contains sticky media containers and an animation system whose `onScroll` reads `window.scrollY` and updates scroll timelines. This supports using native page scroll and persistent media for Labcast; it is not a claim that every Apple component uses the same implementation. Apple's [scroll-view guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/scroll-views) also calls for standard gestures and keyboard behavior. Labcast uses illustrated media, not video footage.

A page-wide CSS halftone adds a dithered comic-book texture without a raster download. The overlay ignores pointer input and disappears in forced-color mode. The approved refinement halves the texture strength, reduces card shadows from 7px to 4px, and reduces rotation from 2° to 0.5°. Featured cards lift only 2px. A bubble fades out before the next begins appearing, keeping one visible at a time. The large artwork, lime headline, and full-width marquee remain the primary visual elements.
